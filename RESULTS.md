# Pilot findings

The normalized-terminal baseline produced $12.25–$20.56 for Case A and $4.99–$7.63 for Case B. Both midpoint comparisons were within three cents of the adviser’s slide. The largest endpoint error was $0.36 in Case A. The published ranges are rounded to dimes, and the inputs in the slides are also rounded. The observed endpoint gaps exceed a pure final-price rounding explanation.

At the high Case A endpoint, substituting 2029 cash flow for normalized terminal cash flow lowered the estimate by $0.77, using year-end timing lowered it by $1.03, and switching from December to March cash and debt increased it by $0.08. The implied net-debt bridge at the midpoint is a $64m reduction from enterprise value to equity in each case.

The main ambiguity worth resolving in each case is how normalized working capital changes across the growth-rate grid. The slides report normalized FCF at the center of each grid and say terminal working capital is a percentage of the change in revenue. The public dollar figures are rounded, and the precise recipe for each grid cell is unclear. Case B is particularly sensitive to whether the terminal year is normalized at all. The estimates and convention sensitivities are scenario calculations.

The cross-deal screening document flags the most direct missing input or audit question observed so far. More filing work is needed before estimating cross-deal reproducibility.

In an exploratory sensitivity added after seeing the endpoint errors, adjusting normalized working capital with 14% of the change in revenue moved the simulated endpoints to $12.39–$20.28 for A and $5.07–$7.48 for B. The maximum absolute gap across all endpoints fell from $0.36 to $0.08. The sensitivity retains the midpoint normalization and proposes a convention for other grid cells.
